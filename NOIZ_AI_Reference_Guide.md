## Voice Prompt Templates

Copy and customize these prompts for your voice design.

### Narration / Documentary

```
A warm, authoritative voice with a knowledgeable presence. Measured pacing with deliberate pauses that invite reflection — like a nature or history documentary narrator.

The delivery is grounded and credible, with subtle breath marks before important transitions that signal something significant is coming.

Perfect for educational content, documentaries, and explainer videos where trust and clarity are paramount.
```

### YouTube Video Intro

```
An energetic, friendly, and welcoming voice that immediately hooks the viewer.

Pacing is slightly faster than conversational to match the excitement of new content. YouTuber-style delivery — approachable and authentic while maintaining professionalism.

The opening sentence gets extra emphasis and energy before settling into a comfortable, engaging rhythm for the rest of the video.
```

### Corporate / Professional

```
A confident, trustworthy, and professional voice that commands respect. Steady pacing with crystal-clear enunciation that leaves no room for ambiguity.

Business presentation style — serious and credible without becoming stiff or robotic.

Tone remains consistent throughout without emotional spikes, delivering information with authority and reliability. Ideal for corporate communications, presentations, and professional training.
```

### Character — Hero / Protagonist

```
A determined, brave, and slightly rough voice that conveys strength and resolve.

Conversational pacing that intensifies during action sequences, adding urgency when the stakes are high. Action hero delivery — commanding presence but grounded in relatability so the audience connects emotionally.

Add natural strain and exertion during emotional peaks to sell the intensity of the moment. Perfect for protagonists who fight against the odds.
```

### Character — Villain / Antagonist

```
A calculated, smooth, and slightly menacing voice that chills and captivates.

Deliberate pacing with pregnant pauses for maximum effect — every word lands with intention. Sophisticated villain delivery — calm, controlled, and threatening without shouting.

Lower register with minimal breath sounds creates an unsettling presence. Perfect for antagonists who are intelligent, dangerous, and terrifyingly composed.
```

### Character — Mentor / Wise Guide

```
A warm, experienced, and reassuring voice that puts the listener at ease.

Slow and thoughtful pacing that invites contemplation — wisdom spoken without haste. Elder mentor delivery — patient, kind, and brimming with hard-earned understanding.

Subtle warmth added to key words and a slight smile detectable in the voice. Perfect for guides, teachers, and characters who offer wisdom and guidance.
```

### Audiobook Fiction

```
A dynamic, expressive, and immersive voice that brings stories to life.

Pacing varies by scene — faster during action and dialogue, slower during moments of introspection and description. Storyteller delivery with the ability to shift into distinct character voices when needed, then seamlessly return to the consistent narrator voice.

Full emotional range while maintaining the through-line of the storytelling. Perfect for fiction that demands versatility and engagement.
```

### Podcast Host

```
A conversational, curious, and engaging voice that feels like talking to a friend.

Natural speech rhythm that mimics authentic human conversation — not too polished, not too casual. Authentic and relaxed delivery that builds rapport with listeners.

Small verbal fillers (um, uh-huh, mm-hm) added naturally to enhance the sense of real conversation. Perfect for podcast hosts, interviewers, and conversational content.
```

### Commercial / Advertisement

```
An upbeat, enthusiastic, and persuasive voice that generates excitement and interest.

Energized, punchy delivery with forward momentum that compels attention. Commercial announcer style — exciting and dynamic while maintaining enough credibility to be trustworthy.

Product names and key benefits get emphasis to drive the message home. Perfect for advertisements, promos, and marketing content that needs to convert.
```

### News / Journalism

```
An objective, serious, and credible voice that delivers information with authority.

Professional broadcast pacing that is steady and measured — never rushed or dragging. News anchor delivery — clear, authoritative, and emotionally neutral.

Minimal emotion with crystal-clear enunciation and a consistent tone throughout. Perfect for news reports, journalism, and informational content where credibility is everything.
```

### Educational / Tutorial

```
A patient, clear, and encouraging voice that supports learning and comprehension.

Moderate pacing with strategic pauses that give learners time to absorb information. Teacher or tutor delivery — supportive, knowledgeable, and approachable without condescension.

Key terms receive emphasis, and complex concepts trigger a slower pace to ensure understanding. Perfect for tutorials, how-to content, and educational videos.
```

### E-Learning / Training

```
A professional, consistent, and approachable voice that maintains engagement over long sessions.

Steady and predictable pacing that creates a reliable rhythm for learners. Corporate trainer delivery — clear, structured, and authoritative without being distant.

Energy is maintained throughout long recordings to prevent listener fatigue. Perfect for corporate training, e-learning courses, and instructional content.
```

## Emotion Combination Recipes

Learn how to blend NOIZ's emotion presets for nuanced, natural-sounding delivery.

### Quick Combos

| Result | Recipe | Best For |
|--------|--------|----------|
| **Warm Welcome** | Happy + Empathetic | Introductions, customer service |
| **Tense Suspense** | Fearful + Curious | Mystery buildups, horror intros |
| **Breaking Bad News** | Sad + Empathetic | Difficult announcements |
| **Triumphant Reveal** | Proud + Excited | Success stories, victories |
| **Frustrated Expert** | Frustrated + Dramatic | Rants, problems needing solving |
| **Genuine Surprise** | Surprise + Delighted | Plot twists, discoveries |
| **Ominous Threat** | Angry + Fearful | Villain dialogue, warnings |
| **Thoughtful Analysis** | Curious + Dramatic | Deep dives, explanations |
| **Envious Rival** | Envious + Frustrated | Competitor dialogue, jealousy |
| **Urgent Alert** | Fearful + Angry | Emergency warnings, calls to action |
| **Joyful Discovery** | Excited + Delighted | Breakthroughs, good news |
| **Heartfelt Speech** | Proud + Empathetic | Tributes, meaningful moments |

### Detailed Examples by Use Case

#### Podcast Intro
> "Welcome back to the show! Today we're diving into something extraordinary..."

**Recipe:** Start with **Excited** to hook listeners, then settle into **Curious + Happy** for the conversational middle. End with **Dramatic** to tease what's coming next.

#### Thriller Prologue
> "The house had been empty for years. Or so everyone thought..."

**Recipe:** **Fearful + Curious** throughout. On the word "thought," switch to **Surprise** briefly before returning to the uneasy base emotions.

#### Customer Service Response
> "I completely understand your frustration, and I'm here to help make this right."

**Recipe:** **Empathetic + Sad** to acknowledge their pain. Avoid **Frustrated** — even if you feel it, the customer shouldn't hear it.

#### Villain Monologue
> "You truly believe you can stop me? How... quaint."

**Recipe:** Start with **Envious + Angry** for the first question. On "quaint," shift to **Dramatic + Fearful** — not fear of the hero, but menace that unsettles.

#### Educational Explainer
> "Here's where things get interesting. Notice how this pattern emerges..."

**Recipe:** **Curious** throughout to model engagement. Switch to **Dramatic** on key insights or "aha" moments to signal importance.

#### Product Launch
> "We've been working toward this moment for three years. Introducing the future of..."

**Recipe:** **Proud + Excited** throughout. The pride signals effort invested; excitement creates contagious energy.

#### Breaking News
> "We're receiving reports of a developing situation downtown. Police are urging everyone to..."

**Recipe:** **Fearful + Dramatic** throughout. Switch briefly to **Surprise** when new information arrives, then return to the urgent base.

#### Personal Story
> "I never thought I'd be standing here today. After everything that happened..."

**Recipe:** Start with **Sad + Empathetic** for the hardship. Transition to **Proud + Delighted** as the story resolves.

#### Rant / Complaint
> "I've tried everything. Nothing works. It's like they designed this to fail!"

**Recipe:** **Frustrated** throughout, building to **Angry** at the peak. Add **Dramatic** on the key complaint for emphasis.

#### Movie Trailer Voice
> "In a world... where nothing is as it seems..."

**Recipe:** **Dramatic + Curious** for the mystery. Layer **Fearful** underneath for tension without panic.

### Pro Tips

1. **Keep it simple** — Two emotions usually work better than three. Too many competing emotions can sound chaotic.

2. **Transitions matter** — When shifting emotions, do it on punctuation marks or sentence breaks for smoother delivery.

3. **Match the content** — If your script is neutral informational, heavy emotion will feel forced. Let the text guide the choice.

4. **Test in chunks** — Generate short 2-3 sentence tests before committing to a long script with complex emotion layering.

5. **Paragraph isolation** — Apply emotions to specific paragraphs to create shifts in tone throughout your script.

## Text-to-Speech Best Practices

### Script Formatting

**Best Practices**

Use punctuation strategically to create natural pauses — periods, commas, and ellipses guide the AI's rhythm. Break long sentences into shorter ones to maintain clarity and prevent rushed delivery. Use paragraph breaks to signal section transitions, allowing the AI to reset between topics. Include phonetic guides for difficult words or unusual names to ensure correct pronunciation. Add emotion tags or direction where the context shifts dramatically.

**Common Pitfalls**

Avoid writing run-on sentences without punctuation — the AI will rush through them or run out of breath. Don't use all caps for extended passages; the AI may interpret this as shouting. Overuse of ellipses can make the delivery sound unnatural and hesitant. Include technical jargon only with pronunciation guides, or the AI will guess incorrectly. Never assume the AI understands sarcasm without explicit direction — sarcasm requires tone that must be specified.

### Punctuation for Expression

Use punctuation strategically to guide the AI's delivery:

**Period (.)** — Full stop with a brief pause. Signals completion of a thought.

**Comma (,)** — Quick pause that indicates more is coming. Keeps the flow moving forward.

**Ellipses (...)** — Longer pause with a trailing-off effect. Suggests uncertainty, hesitation, or a dramatic transition.

**Em-dash (—)** — Abrupt change or interruption. Creates emphasis or signals a shift in thought.

**Question mark (?)** — Raises pitch at end. Indicates inquiry or uncertainty.

**Exclamation point (!)** — Adds energy and emphasis. Use sparingly for maximum impact.

**Quotation marks (" ")** — Suggests a change in tone when speaking quoted words. Can indicate irony, dialogue, or emphasis.

### Adding Emphasis

**CAPITALIZATION**
```
This is REALLY important.
```
Adds emphasis on "really" — the AI will give the word more energy and weight. Use sparingly for maximum impact.

**Italics and Strategic Formatting**
```
This is *critically* important.
```
Slight emphasis that sounds more natural than all caps. The word gets emphasis without feeling forced.

**Contextual Direction Tags**
```
(whispering) This is our only chance.
(shouting) Get down now!
(sarcastic) Oh, brilliant plan.
```
Parenthetical direction tells the AI exactly how to deliver the line. Use for major shifts in delivery that punctuation alone can't convey.

### Handling Difficult Words

**Technical Terms**

Acronyms and abbreviations can trip up AI. Write them phonetically when needed:
```
A.I. (ay-eye) — not AI as one syllable
2024 (twenty twenty-four) — not 2-0-2-4
CEO (see-ee-oh) — not see-oh
```

**Foreign Words**

Provide phonetic pronunciation in brackets when introducing unfamiliar terms:
```
"The concept of joie de vivre [zhwa duh veevrah] transforms how we approach daily life."
```

**Names**

Add pronunciation guides for uncommon names to avoid awkward mispronunciations:
```
"Siobhan [shi-vawn] arrived early."
"X Æ A-12 [ex-ay-twelve] is a unique name."
```
